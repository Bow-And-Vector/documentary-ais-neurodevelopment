# Collaborator and interview outreach packet

## Project title
The Binary Myth

## One-sentence project description
A documentary exploring the science of prenatal sex development, androgen insensitivity syndrome, and the developing brain, and how simplistic ideas about male and female are being used to justify restrictions on gender-diverse care in Texas and beyond.

## Why this project matters
This project tells a story at the intersection of science, embodiment, and public policy. It examines how real developmental biology complicates simplistic ideas about sex and gender, and why those oversimplifications are now shaping medical decisions for young people.

## Project thesis
Human development is not reducible to a binary framework. Androgen insensitivity syndrome, receptor variation, and prenatal hormone effects on the developing brain are all evidence that biological variation is real, medically meaningful, and often ignored in public policy.

## Who this film is for
- general audiences interested in science and social issues
- students of medicine, neuroscience, and developmental biology
- policy watchers and advocates
- communities affected by medical restrictions and social misunderstanding
- viewers interested in how science and public discourse interact

## The documentary’s core arguments
- Sex development is a process shaped by timing, hormones, and receptor function.
- AIS and other forms of developmental variation demonstrate the limits of a binary understanding of sex.
- Prenatal hormone exposure can shape the developing brain, but the science is nuanced and not reducible to simple stereotypes.
- Public policy based on a false biological certainty can cause harm.
- The film aims to encourage a more scientifically literate public conversation about sex, development, and care.

## What the film will show
- the science of sex differentiation and fetal development
- the role of androgen signaling and receptor variation
- the medical and social realities of AIS and related conditions
- lived experience and personal stories of people affected by developmental variation
- the policy consequences of denying that variation exists
- the ethical stakes of forcing rigid categories onto complex biology
