# Documentary Project: AIS, Brain Development, and the Texas Policy Debate

This repository is the starting point for a documentary examining prenatal neurodevelopment, androgen signaling, and the experiences of people with partial or mild androgen insensitivity syndrome (AIS), with a focus on how that science intersects with current debates over gender-affirming care in Texas and across the United States.

## Project thesis

The documentary explores how fetal sex differentiation and androgen exposure can produce a body and a brain that do not map neatly onto a simple male/female binary. It asks what happens when scientific understanding of sex development, neurodevelopment, and endocrine variation collides with policy frameworks that insist on rigid categories.

This work is designed to be rigorous, source-based, and transparent. The repository is structured to support:

- research and citation tracking
- interview planning and transcription
- script development and narrative structure
- visual explainer creation
- production planning
- public-facing website or archive pages

## Why this project matters

This documentary is not simply about gender identity in the abstract. It is about the scientific reality that sex development is not reducible to a single binary model. The science of androgen insensitivity, receptor variability, and prenatal hormone exposure suggests that developmental variation can create complex combinations of physiology, cognition, and self-understanding.

At the same time, a public policy environment shaped by oversimplified beliefs about sex and gender can produce harmful consequences for children and families—particularly where medical treatment decisions are restricted without regard for developmental and endocrine science.

The project aims to make these issues understandable without flattening them into political slogans. It is intended to serve as both a documentary narrative and an educational archive.

## Research principles

- cite all factual claims in the research folder
- distinguish between established science and interpretation
- avoid treating lived experience as anecdotal proof without context
- clearly separate medical knowledge from policy claims
- be careful with terminology and use the language preferred by the people being represented

6. Maintain production docs in `production/`.

## Suggested editorial direction

The documentary can be structured around a few key arcs:

- fetal development and endocrine signaling
- what AIS and receptor variation reveal about sex and development
- how those findings challenge simplistic legal frameworks
- the lived experiences of affected people
- the policy consequences in Texas and beyond

## Starter research prompts

Use these themes when collecting source material:

- prenatal androgen exposure and brain development
- androgen receptor variation and receptor sensitivity
- partial and mild AIS in biological and social context
- how sex development is studied in human medicine
- brain dimorphism debates and evidence quality
- state policy around gender-affirming care for minors
- ethical and legal issues in medical treatment restrictions

## Notes for contributors

As the project evolves, keep in mind:

- This is a documentary and an educational resource, not just a political argument.
- You should preserve nuance without diluting clarity.
- The story should honor both scientific evidence and lived experience.
- The work should be accessible to general audiences while remaining credible to experts.
