# Source map and working bibliography

This document is the working research plan for the documentary. It is designed to keep the story grounded in evidence while still remaining accessible for a general audience.

## Core documentary question

How does the science of prenatal sex differentiation and androgen signaling challenge the idea that sex and gender are reducible to a simple male/female binary, and how does that science intersect with contemporary policy debates over care for gender-diverse youth in Texas and beyond?

## Research strategy

The documentary should build in three layers:

1. Biological and developmental science
2. Human experience and identity
3. Public policy and medical decision-making

Each layer should be supported by separate source sets and each source should be tagged by confidence, recency, and relevance.

## 1) Biological and developmental science

### A. Foundational sex differentiation literature

Priority sources:

- standard endocrinology and developmental biology textbooks
- review articles on sex determination and sexual differentiation
- human embryology references explaining gonadal development and external genital development
- genetics and endocrine references on androgen receptor function

Key topics:

- XY sex determination and development
- gonadal differentiation and fetal endocrine signaling
- androgen receptor physiology
- androgen sensitivity and receptor variation
- disorders/differences of sex development (DSD)

Source examples to gather:

- endocrinology reviews on the role of androgens in fetal differentiation
- medical literature on the androgen receptor and receptor polymorphisms
- DSD clinical reviews on AIS and related conditions

### B. AIS-specific literature

Priority sources:

- clinical review articles on complete, partial, and mild AIS
- pediatric endocrinology guidelines
- genetics and reproductive endocrinology papers
- literature explaining patient variability in phenotype and developmental trajectories

Key questions:

- What is the spectrum of AIS severity?
- What causes partial or mild forms?
- How is diagnosis made, and how variable are outcomes?
- What does the literature say about phenotype, body development, and psychosocial experience?

### C. Brain development and prenatal hormone exposure

Priority sources:

- neuroscience review articles on prenatal hormone exposure and brain development
- developmental neuroscience papers on sex differences in brain organization
- literature discussing the limits of oversimplifying brain sex differences
- studies examining the role of hormones during critical developmental windows

Key questions:

- What is the evidence for prenatal hormone effects on the brain?
- At what stages of development do hormones matter most?
- What are the strengths and limits of this research?
- How do scientists distinguish between robust findings and speculative interpretations?

## 2) Human experience and identity

Priority sources:

- interviews with people who have AIS or related developmental variations
- family narratives and lived-experience accounts
- patient advocacy organizations and support communities
- psychology or social science work on identity, embodiment, and developmental variation

Key questions:

- How do people describe their bodies, gender identities, and medical experiences?
- What do they say about uncertainty, misdiagnosis, or misunderstanding?
- How does the science of variation interact with personal identity?

Important note:

Use the language preferred by the people whose experiences are being represented. Do not flatten personal narratives into medical categories alone.

## 3) Policy and public health context

### A. Texas policy and legal landscape

Priority sources:

- Texas legislative bills and amendments restricting medical treatment for gender-diverse youth
- official statements from Texas agencies or policymakers
- court filings, legal review documents, and constitutional/rights arguments
- reporting from reputable news organizations covering the issue
- medical association guidance on gender-affirming care for minors

Key questions:

- What legal frameworks have been used to justify restrictions?
- How are medical and scientific assumptions being used in policy arguments?
- How do restrictions affect access to care, family decision-making, and clinician practice?

### B. National policy context

Priority sources:

- state-level restrictions in other jurisdictions
- policy briefs and rights-focused summaries
- medical association statements and treatment guidance
- public health and ethics writing on care for transgender and gender-diverse youth

Key questions:

- How does the Texas debate relate to national policy trends?
- How are scientific concerns raised or dismissed in public argument?
- Where do policy debates reflect misunderstanding of developmental variation versus legitimate disagreement on standards of care?

## Source organization template

For each source, record the following:

- Topic
- Source title
- Author(s) or organization
- Type (review, clinical guideline, policy document, interview, news report)
- Year
- Link / DOI / document identifier
- Summary of key takeaway
- Relevance to documentary arc
- Status: unverified / verified / used in script

## Suggested priority source types to collect first

1. Review articles on fetal sex differentiation and endocrine development
2. Clinical literature on AIS and androgen receptor function
3. Neuroscience reviews on prenatal hormone effects and brain organization
4. Patient narratives from AIS communities and support groups
5. Texas legislative texts and legal analysis
6. Medical association statements on care for gender-diverse youth
7. Reputable reporting on the policy landscape and its consequences

## Practical research questions to guide the first pass

- What is the consensus view on fetal androgen exposure and the developing body?
- What is the consensus view on fetal androgen exposure and the developing brain?
- What does the literature on AIS show about variability and complexity?
- Which policy documents in Texas best illustrate the public misunderstanding of sex development?
- Which medical sources are most useful to explain why sex development is not a simple binary?

## Working bibliography categories

### Scientific / medical

- fetal development and sex differentiation
- androgen receptor and signaling biology
- AIS clinical reviews and case literature
- developmental neuroscience and hormone effects
- DSD and pediatric endocrinology guidance

### Human story / community

- interviews with people living with AIS or related variations
- family accounts and support group testimony
- narratives about identity, medical care, and social misunderstanding

### Policy / law

- Texas legal and legislative documents
- state executive branch documents or guidance
- national policy analysis and legal commentary
- public health ethics analysis of transgender and gender-diverse care restrictions
