# Interview subject map

## Primary subject categories

### 1. Clinical endocrinology and AIS / DSD care
- pediatric endocrinologist with AIS or DSD expertise
- clinical geneticist or molecular biologist studying androgen receptor variation
- reproductive endocrinologist working with disorders of sex development

Why they matter:
- they can explain the range of AIS phenotypes and the clinical importance of receptor variation
- they can clarify why the science is a spectrum and not a binary script

### 2. Developmental neuroscience
- neuroscientist studying prenatal hormone exposure and brain development
- developmental psychologist or brain scientist working on sex differentiation and neural organization

Why they matter:
- they can explain the legitimate science behind prenatal hormonal influence without overstating it
- they can offer a measured response to common oversimplifications in public discourse

### 3. Lived experience and self-definition
- person with partial or mild AIS
- person whose body and identity challenge easy binary assumptions
- parent or family member of a person with AIS or DSD
- counselor or advocate working with affected people

Why they matter:
- they provide the emotional and human dimension of the film
- they show how embodiment and social expectation can conflict

### 4. Texas policy and legal analysis
- policy analyst or legal scholar on Texas medical restrictions for minors
- reporter who has covered the law and its impact in Texas
- advocate or attorney working with affected families

Why they matter:
- they can explain how the law is framed and enforced
- they can show the policy implications of medical misinformation and simplification

### 5. Youth medicine and gender-diverse care
- pediatrician or clinician who treats transgender and gender-diverse youth
- psychologist or mental health professional involved in informed care
- medical ethicist focused on informed consent and evidence-based care

Why they matter:
- they can explain how care decisions are made in practice
- they can highlight differences between evidence-based care and political restriction

## Priority interview list (recommended order)

1. Pediatric endocrinologist with AIS or DSD expertise
2. Geneticist or molecular biologist focused on androgen receptor mutation and phenotype variation
3. Developmental neuroscientist studying prenatal hormone exposure and the brain
4. Person with partial or mild AIS
5. Parent or family member of a person with AIS or related developmental variation
6. Policy analyst or legal expert on Texas restrictions
7. Pediatrician or clinician working with gender-diverse youth
8. Advocate or counselor supporting families impacted by policy restrictions

## Suggested interview question themes

- How do you define and explain the spectrum of AIS?
- What is most misunderstood about sex development in public discourse?
- How do androgens and androgen receptors influence developmental outcomes?
- What does the brain science actually support—and what does it not support?
- How do clinical decisions get distorted by rigid gender assumptions?
- What are the effects of policy restrictions on youth and families?
- What does medically informed, respectful care look like in practice?

## Interview strategy note

Use a consistent framework across all interviews:

- establish scientific background
- invite personal experience
- ask what the public gets wrong
- leave room for nuance and uncertainty
- avoid turning any single person into a spokesperson for an entire population
