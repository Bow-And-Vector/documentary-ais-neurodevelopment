# Expert Interview Questions

## Use these as guides for your expert outreach

Send this to each expert you contact so they know exactly what you're asking about.

---

## EXPERT 1: Pediatric endocrinologist or DSD specialist

**Duration: 15-20 minutes**

**Opening:**
"I'm making a short documentary for Texas voters before early voting begins. I'd like to understand the science from an expert perspective. Here are the questions I'd like to ask:"

**Questions:**

1. Can you explain what androgen insensitivity syndrome is, and why it matters clinically?

2. How common is AIS, and what's the spectrum of presentations—from complete to partial to mild?

3. What does the androgen receptor actually do in fetal development?

4. How does variation in androgen receptor function create different developmental outcomes?

5. If someone asks you 'is sex binary or not,' what would you say as a clinician?

6. What do you think the public most often misunderstands about sex development?

7. How does your medical training prepare you to handle cases where development doesn't fit binary categories?

8. If a state government passed a law saying sex is only male or female, and doctors can't treat variation—what would that mean for your practice?

**Target soundbites:**
- Short, clear, quotable (15-30 seconds each)
- Focus on clarity, not jargon
- If they use technical terms, ask them to explain

## EXPERT 2: Neuroscientist or developmental biologist

**Duration: 15-20 minutes**

**Opening:**
"I'm making a documentary about how prenatal hormone exposure affects brain development. I want to understand what the science actually shows—and what it doesn't show."

**Questions:**

1. What does the research tell us about how prenatal androgens affect brain development?

2. At what stages of development do hormones matter most for the brain?

3. Is there a 'male brain' and a 'female brain,' or is that oversimplified?

4. If someone has atypical androgen exposure—like in partial AIS—what might that mean for brain organization?

5. How much do we actually know about this, and how much remains uncertain?

6. What do you think scientists get wrong when they talk about sex and the brain to the general public?

7. Can prenatal hormone effects on the brain explain identity and behavior? What can and can't they explain?

8. If you had to explain this to a high school student, what would be the most important thing they should know?

**Target soundbites:**
- Honest about what we know and don't know
- Avoids oversimplification
- Accessible language

## EXPERT 3: Clinician or policy expert on Texas restrictions

**Duration: 15-20 minutes**

**Opening:**
"I'm making a documentary about Texas policy on gender-affirming care. I'd like to understand how this policy works and what the medical implications are."

**Questions:**

1. Can you explain what SB 14 actually restricts? What's prohibited and what's still allowed?

2. What's the medical and scientific basis for restrictions like this?

3. What do major medical organizations (AAP, AMA, Endocrine Society) actually say about gender-affirming care for youth?

4. How does SB 14 affect how you practice medicine or how clinicians in Texas practice?

5. What happens to kids and families when access to care is restricted?

6. What would you want Texas voters to understand about this issue before they vote?

7. If someone tells you "sex is only male or female," how do you respond as a medical professional?

8. What's the relationship between understanding developmental biology and making good policy?

**Target soundbites:**
- Facts and evidence
- Personal impact stories
- Clear explanation of policy implications

## FILMING THE INTERVIEWS

**Technical setup:**
- Zoom call (with recording permission)
- Use OBS Studio to record both video and audio
- Or use Zoom's built-in recording feature
- Test audio before you start
- Ensure good lighting on your expert (they're on camera too)

**During the interview:**
- Let them finish their thoughts
- If they use jargon, ask them to explain
- Be genuinely curious—this is a conversation, not an interrogation
- Don't rush them
- Ask follow-up questions if something is unclear

**After the interview:**
- Thank them
- Ask if they want to review their footage before it's used
- Collect their name, credentials, affiliation for credits
- Export clean audio + video files

---

## WHAT YOU'RE LOOKING FOR

**Best soundbites are:**
- Clear and quotable
- 15-45 seconds long (can cut to that length)
- Don't require heavy editing or context
- Show genuine expertise
- Communicate to a general audience

**Avoid:**
- Long rambling answers
- Heavy jargon without explanation
- "Um," "uh," and filler words (you can edit these out)
- Anything libelous or actionable
