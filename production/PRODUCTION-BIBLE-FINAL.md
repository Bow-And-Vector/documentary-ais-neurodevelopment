## FILM STRUCTURE (Scene-by-scene breakdown)

### SCENE 1: OPENING (0:00-0:30)
**Duration:** 30 seconds
**Tone:** Urgent, personal, direct

**VISUAL:**
- Black screen, 2 seconds
- Fade in: extreme close-up of your face, looking directly at camera

- Text appears on screen: "A story about science, Texas, and what's being forbidden"

**AUDIO:**

"My name is [Your Name]. I'm from College Station, Texas. I have something to tell you about my body, my brain, and why the government is trying to stop doctors from talking about it."

**PRODUCTION NOTES:**
- Shoot in soft natural light (window light is ideal)
- Wear something that feels authentically you
- Make direct eye contact with camera
- Let the camera sit on your face for 2-3 seconds of silence
- This is your contract with the viewer: "I'm telling you the truth"

### SCENE 2: THE BODY (0:30-1:15)
**Duration:** 45 seconds
**Tone:** Educational, personal, grounded

**VISUAL:**
- You on camera (medium shot, from shoulders up)
- B-roll overlay: abstract medical imagery or simple graphics showing:
  - Fetal development timeline (8 weeks → 20 weeks → birth)
  - Simple animation of androgens/hormone molecules
  - Androgen receptor illustration
- Text overlays:
  - "Hypospadias: a developmental variation"
  - "Androgen receptors shape the body AND the brain"

**AUDIO:**
Your voice-over:
"When I was born, I had hypospadias. It's a developmental variation—the way my body developed in the womb didn't follow a simple script. That's not rare. It's not abnormal. It's biological variation.

But here's what matters: prenatal hormones, particularly androgens, shape not just the body. They shape the brain.

This is established neuroscience. This is not controversial. This is medical fact."

**PRODUCTION NOTES:**
- Film yourself speaking this section in a quiet room
- You can read from notes or memorize; authenticity matters more than perfection
- Pacing: slow and clear, allowing each point to land
- B-roll (animation/graphics) can be sourced or created (see animation requirements below)

### SCENE 3: THE BRAIN (1:15-2:15)
**Duration:** 60 seconds
**Tone:** Intimate, vulnerable, scientifically grounded

**VISUAL:**
- You on camera, slightly more vulnerable expression
- B-roll: soft, intimate moments of you in your home
  - Hands, reflective moments, looking out windows
  - Simple brain illustration or neural network animation
  - "Mosaic brain" concept visualization
- Text overlays:
  - "At age [X], I discovered something about myself"
  - "A latent part of my neurobiology"
  - "My dopamine response was real"

**AUDIO:**
Your voice, more intimate:
"When I was in my twenties, I got sick. Lyme disease, mold, POTS. I had to wear compression stockings.

One day, I put on women's compression stockings. And when I looked in the mirror, something in my nervous system responded. A part of my brain lit up.

That wasn't a choice. It wasn't ideology. It was neurological. It was my brain showing me something about itself that I didn't know existed.

I spent years exploring that. Learning about my own neurobiology. That is the core of this story. That is what the government in Texas is now trying to make illegal: the ability to understand your own brain."

**PRODUCTION NOTES:**
- This is the emotional center of the film
- Film multiple takes; let yourself feel what you're describing
- B-roll should be intimate but not melodramatic
- The compression stockings moment can be shown as simple, quiet imagery
- Let silence do work; you don't need constant narration

**Duration:** 60 seconds
**Tone:** Harder, more direct, political

And when the government passes laws based on false certainty, real people get hurt. Real families suffer. Real doctors are criminalized.

On November 5th, Texas votes for a governor who will either continue these restrictions or reject them. And on October 15th, early voting begins.

Before you vote, I want you to understand: this is not about politics. This is about whether we're willing to be honest about how the human body and brain actually work."

**PRODUCTION NOTES:**
- This section needs to feel urgent but not hysterical
- Direct eye contact with camera
- Policy clips should be factual and from official sources (public record, news footage)
- Text overlays should be clear and readable

## AUDIO AND SOUND DESIGN

### Voiceover recording

**What to record:**
- Your testimony (script provided in YOUR-TESTIMONY-SCRIPT.md)
- Multiple takes of each section
- Quiet room, good microphone
- Export as uncompressed WAV or MP3

**How to record:**
- Use your phone's voice memos or a free tool like Audacity
- Speak clearly, naturally, not overly performed
- Include pauses where indicated in script
- Record at consistent volume

### Music/ambient sound

**Style:** Minimal, emotional, not distracting
**Options:**
- Royalty-free from YouTube Audio Library (free)
- Creative Commons from Epidemic Sound or Artlist (free versions available)
- Instrumental, contemplative, science-forward vibe
- Recommendation: search "documentary background music" for the right tone

**Volume levels:**
- Your voiceover: -12dB to -6dB (clear and present)
- Background music: -24dB to -18dB (underneath, not dominant)
- Sound effects (if any): -15dB to -9dB (supportive)

### Subtitles/Captions

**CRITICAL for social media:** Videos without captions lose 80% of viewers

**How to create:**
1. Export your finished video from Resolve
2. Use Audacity or Whisper to auto-transcribe your audio
3. Create SRT subtitle file
4. Add to video in Resolve or YouTube
5. Adjust timing and formatting for readability

**Format:**
- White text, black outline
- Large enough to read on phone
- Synchronized with speech
- Include speaker identification if multiple people

## EDITING WORKFLOW (DaVinci Resolve 21.1)

### Project settings

**Timeline resolution:** 1080p (1920x1080) or 4K (3840x2160)
**Frame rate:** 24fps or 30fps (choose one and stick with it)
**Audio sample rate:** 48kHz
**Audio channels:** Stereo

### Editing structure

**Track layout:**
- V1: Testimony and B-roll (main video)
- V2: Graphics/animations overlay
- V3: Text overlays and titles
- A1: Voiceover
- A2: Music/ambience
- A3: Sound effects or interview audio

### Basic workflow

1. **Import media**
   - File > Import Media
   - Select all footage, graphics, audio
   - Organize into bins: Testimony, B-roll, Graphics, Audio

2. **Rough assembly**
   - Drag clips to timeline in order
   - Sync voiceover with clips
   - Get rough timing right

3. **Refine cuts**
   - Trim clips to remove pauses, mistakes
   - Adjust pacing
   - Add transitions if needed (usually not; cuts are cleaner)

4. **Add graphics/animations**
   - Layer animations over testimony
   - Sync timing with script
   - Adjust opacity if needed

5. **Add text**
   - Create text overlay for titles, info
   - Use consistent font and size
   - Ensure readability on small screens

6. **Color grade**
   - Adjust exposure and white balance
   - Make consistent across all footage
   - Warm, intimate feel (not clinical)
   - Use Resolve's built-in LUTs if needed

7. **Sound design**
   - Layer music and voiceover
   - Adjust levels (see Audio section above)
   - EQ if needed
   - Leave 1-2 seconds of silence at beginning and end

8. **Add subtitles**
   - Import SRT file or create in Resolve
   - Sync with audio
   - Adjust formatting for readability

9. **Export**
   - File > Export
   - Format: H.264 (for online) or ProRes (for archival)
   - Resolution: 1080p or 4K
   - Bitrate: 8-16 Mbps for H.264
   - Audio: AAC stereo, 128 kbps

## TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS

### For YouTube upload

**Format:** H.264 video codec, AAC audio
**Resolution:** 1080p (1920x1080) recommended
**Frame rate:** 24fps or 30fps
**Bitrate:** 8-16 Mbps video, 128 kbps audio
**File size:** ~50-200 MB for a 5-minute video
**Maximum:** YouTube accepts up to 256 GB per video

### For TikTok/Instagram

**Format:** H.264 or HEVC
**Resolution:** 1080p vertical (1080x1920) or 1080p square (1080x1080)
**Frame rate:** 30fps
**Aspect ratio:** 9:16 (vertical) for TikTok, 1:1 (square) for Instagram Reels
**Duration:** up to 10 minutes
**File size:** Smaller is better; keep under 50 MB if possible

### For archive (your GitHub repo)

**Format:** ProRes 422 HQ (for best quality) or H.264 (for smaller file size)
**Resolution:** 4K (3840x2160) if you filmed in 4K
**Frame rate:** Match your original footage
**Bitrate:** High (ProRes) or medium (H.264)
**Purpose:** Archival, future use, insurance against loss

## PRODUCTION NOTES & REMINDERS

### Key success factors

1. **Authenticity over perfection**
   - Your genuine emotion matters more than technical polish
   - Imperfect is often more powerful than perfect
   - Let yourself be vulnerable

2. **Science is the spine**
   - Every claim needs to be grounded in evidence
   - Graphics should be accurate
   - Expert soundbites should be quotable

3. **Time is the enemy**
   - Do not get stuck perfecting one scene
   - A finished 3-minute film beats an unfinished 7-minute film
   - Ship it by October 14, even if not perfect

4. **Pacing is everything**
   - Vary shot lengths to keep viewer engaged
   - Use music and sound to guide emotion
   - Leave breathing room; don't over-explain

5. **Subtitles are non-negotiable**
   - 80% of social media is watched without audio
   - Captions make the film accessible
   - It's easy to add; do it

### What could go wrong (and how to handle it)

**Problem:** Expert won't commit to interview
**Solution:** Use the scientist soundbites you already have or script voice-over with animation

**Problem:** B-roll doesn't look good
**Solution:** Use more graphics/animation to reduce reliance on B-roll

**Problem:** Graphics take too long
**Solution:** Source existing scientific animations online (with proper attribution) or use simple static images

**Problem:** Edit takes longer than expected
**Solution:** Prioritize core sections (your testimony + policy + call to action); cut optional sections

**Problem:** You're not happy with your on-camera performance
**Solution:** Re-shoot or record voiceover instead; authenticity > perfection
